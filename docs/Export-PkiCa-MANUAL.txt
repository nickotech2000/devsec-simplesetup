====================================================================================================
 1  WHAT EXPORT-PKICA DOES
====================================================================================================
 A CDN (Cloudflare, Azure Front Door / CDN, CloudFront, Alibaba CDN, Google Cloud CDN, Akamai), a
 reverse proxy or a load balancer stands between the visitor and your server. It opens its own
 connection to your server (the "origin") and checks the certificate it finds there. It knows the
 public CAs. It does NOT know your private PKI, so it refuses your certificate until you give it
 your CA certificate. Cloudflare calls this "Bring Your Own CA". The others have similar forms.

 Export-PkiCa writes the CA certificates in the formats and with the names those forms accept. It
 writes PUBLIC certificates. A private key goes out only if you ask (-IncludeKey).

 It also works the other way. A customer has a certificate (from your PKI or a public CA) and asks
 "which CA files do I give the CDN?". Give the script the certificate with -Leaf. It follows the
 chain and writes the intermediates and the root.

====================================================================================================
 2  THE WORDS YOU NEED
====================================================================================================
 CA            Certificate authority. The part of the PKI that signs certificates.
 Root          The top CA. Everything below it is trusted because the root is trusted.
 Intermediate  A CA between the root and your server certificate (DV, OV, EV).
 Leaf          The certificate of your server. It is at the bottom of the chain.
 Chain         Leaf, then intermediate(s), then root.
 CDN           A service that sits in front of your site and serves it from many places.
 Origin        Your own server behind the CDN.
 PEM / DER     Two ways to write the same certificate: text, or binary.
 Quadrant      Q1 = Classic, not FIPS. Q2 = Classic, FIPS. Q3 = Quantum, not FIPS.
               Q4 = Quantum, FIPS.
 NoCR          A PKI whose CA certificates have no CRL / OCSP address inside.
 More words: GLOSSARY.txt and GLOSSARY-ENGLISH.txt.

====================================================================================================
 3  BEFORE YOU START
====================================================================================================
 1. You have the PKI folder (made by pki_setup.ps1), or a certificate file from a customer.
 2. PowerShell 7 and OpenSSL 4 are installed. Run .\Prepare-Computer.ps1 -Check if unsure.
 3. You know what the service wants: only the root, or the root and every intermediate. When you
    are not sure, give all of them (-WithPath).
 4. Decide: do you need a key? Almost always NO. A CDN needs only the certificate.

====================================================================================================
 4  QUICK START
====================================================================================================
     .\Export-PkiCa.ps1 -List                       what can I choose?
     .\Export-PkiCa.ps1                             all roots, all formats, Cloudflare names
     .\Export-PkiCa.ps1 -Ca q1-ov -WithPath         Q1 OV CA and all parents to the Enterprise Root
     .\Export-PkiCa.ps1 -Leaf .\www-fullchain.crt -Target All

 The result is in a new folder under  transport\ . Open its README.txt first. A folder is never
 overwritten. Every run makes a new one. Use -OutFolder to choose another place.

====================================================================================================
 5  CHOOSING THE CAs (-Ca)
====================================================================================================
 Default is -Ca Roots. You can give several values with commas, for example -Ca q1-root,q1-ov.

   Value                What you get
   -------------------  ---------------------------------------------------------------------
   Roots                every root: the Enterprise Root, the track roots, the quadrant roots
   EnterpriseRoot       only the Enterprise Root
   TrackRoots           the Classic root and the Quantum root
   QuadrantRoots        the Q1 .. Q4 roots
   Intermediates        every DV, OV and EV issuing CA
   Classic | Quantum    every CA of that track
   Q1 | Q2 | Q3 | Q4    the quadrant root and its DV / OV / EV CAs
   DV | OV | EV         that kind of intermediate in every quadrant
   All                  every CA
   q1-ov, q3-root ...   one CA by its id (the ids are shown by -List)

 -WithPath      Also add every parent CA up to the Enterprise Root. Each CA keeps its own files.
                Example: -Ca q1-ov -WithPath = Enterprise Root + Classic Root + Q1 Root + Q1 OV.
 -BothVariants  Do it for the normal PKI (CRL / OCSP addresses inside) AND the NoCR PKI. Without
                it, one PKI is used: the newest release, or the NoCR one with -NoCaRevocationInfo.
                -Release <n> picks an older release.
 -List          Print the CAs you can pick (id, kind, key, validity) and stop.
 CA ids: root, classic-root, quantum-root, q1-root, q1-dv, q1-ov, q1-ev, q2-.. q3-.. q4-..

====================================================================================================
 6  FORMATS (-Format)
====================================================================================================
 -Format takes a list of PEM CRT CER DER P7B TXT, or ALL (default).
   PEM, CRT   Text with -----BEGIN CERTIFICATE-----. Linux, nginx, Apache, most clouds.
   CER, DER   Binary form of the same certificate. Windows, Azure, Java.
   P7B        PKCS#7 container (certificates only). Windows, IIS.
   TXT        The certificate as readable text (openssl x509 -text).

====================================================================================================
 7  CDN AND CLOUD NAMES (-Target)
====================================================================================================
 -Target (default Cloudflare): Cloudflare AzureCdn CloudFront AlibabaCdn GoogleCdn Akamai All None.
 For each target a copy of every certificate is written with the service in the name:

   Target       Files (for a CA called agantra-root-g1)
   -----------  -------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   Cloudflare   agantra-root-g1.cloudflare.pem   .cloudflare.crt
   AzureCdn     agantra-root-g1.azure.pem   .azure.crt   .azure.cer
   CloudFront   agantra-root-g1.cloudfront.pem   .cloudfront.crt
   AlibabaCdn   agantra-root-g1.alibaba.pem   .alibaba.crt
   GoogleCdn    agantra-root-g1.google.pem   .google.crt
   Akamai       agantra-root-g1.akamai.pem   .akamai.crt

 Each file holds ONE certificate in PEM text, with LF line ends and nothing before the BEGIN line.
 This is what the upload forms accept. Files with all selected CAs are also written:
 ca-bundle.pem and ca-bundle.<target>.pem.

====================================================================================================
 8  KEYS (-IncludeKey)
====================================================================================================
 The default is NO key. With -IncludeKey, every CA whose key is on this computer also gets
   <name>.key.pem    the private key, ENCRYPTED (PKCS#8, AES-256)
   <name>.pfx        key + certificate + chain, same password
 -KeyPassword <text> sets the password. Without it a random one is made and shown ONCE on the
 screen. Write it down. The Enterprise Root key also needs -Force. A DevKit has no root keys, so
 they are skipped.

 Think before you use it. A CA key is the master key of a part of your PKI. A CDN needs only the
 CA CERTIFICATE. Use keys only to move a CA to another system you control. Do not e-mail the
 folder. Do not copy a CA .key to other machines for convenience. Keep the folder like a safe.

====================================================================================================
 9  START FROM A LEAF CERTIFICATE (-Leaf)
====================================================================================================
     .\Export-PkiCa.ps1 -Leaf .\www.example.com-fullchain.crt -Target All
     .\Export-PkiCa.ps1 -Leaf .\shop.pfx -LeafPassword 'Abc12345!' -FetchAia -NoPki
     .\Export-PkiCa.ps1 -Leaf .\www.crt -LeafKey .\www.key -IncludeKey

   -Leaf <file>       .pem .crt .cer .der, a file that already has the chain, .p7b, .pfx / .p12.
   -LeafKey <file>    Optional private key of the leaf. The script proves it belongs to the leaf.
   -LeafPassword      Password of the .pfx and / or of an encrypted key.
   -ChainFile <list>  Extra files with intermediate or root certificates.
   -FetchAia          Download a missing intermediate from the "CA Issuers" address in the
                      certificate. Needs the network. Off by default.
   -NoPki             Do not look for issuers in this project's own PKI.

 How it finds the chain: it looks for the certificate whose subject equals the issuer of the leaf
 (and whose key identifier matches), then does the same for that one, up to a root. It looks in
 the leaf file, in -ChainFile files, in this PKI, and (with -FetchAia) on the internet. If a link
 is missing, it names it. The leaf is written too (certificate only). With -IncludeKey and
 -LeafKey you also get an encrypted .pfx of the leaf.

 The output folder name tells you where it came from:
   ca-export_pki-default-01_roots_nokey_cloudflare_20261017-113000
   ca-export_leaf-www-example-com_chain_nokey_all_20261017-113500

====================================================================================================
 10  WHERE DOES EACH SERVICE WANT THE FILE?
====================================================================================================
 The README.txt of each export repeats this. Menus change. Check the current documentation of the
 service.
   Cloudflare   Origin authentication with your own CA ("Bring Your Own CA", Authenticated Origin
                Pulls, client certificates in API Shield / mTLS): upload the .cloudflare.pem.
   Azure        Front Door / Application Gateway / CDN origin or client trust: the .azure.cer (or
                .pem) of the root and each intermediate.
   CloudFront   Origin mutual TLS and the trust store: the .cloudfront.pem files, or one bundle
                (ca-bundle.cloudfront.pem).
   Alibaba CDN  Origin and client certificate trust in the console: the .alibaba.pem.
   Google       Certificate Manager trust configs (TrustConfig) for mTLS: the .google.pem of the
                root and the intermediates.
   Akamai       Property Manager "Custom Certificate Authority Set": the .akamai.pem files.
 Give the service the ROOT and every INTERMEDIATE between the root and your server certificate
 (use -WithPath), unless the service says it wants the root only.

====================================================================================================
 11  WARNINGS ON THE SCREEN
====================================================================================================
 The files are still written when you see these.
   ML-DSA (post-quantum) CA   CDNs and public clouds take RSA and ECDSA only. Use Classic CAs.
   RSA-8192 CA                Many services limit RSA to 4096. Use the Q1 Root or an intermediate.
   Expired CA                 The date has passed. Issue a new CA first.
   Not a CA certificate       The file is a leaf. A CDN refuses it as a trust anchor.

====================================================================================================
 12  HASH FILES
====================================================================================================
 -Hash (default All) writes a hash file for every file, in the format of sha256sum:

     <hash>  <file name>          check with:  sha256sum -c agantra-root-g1.sha256

   All   SHA-256, SHA-384, SHA-512 (name.sha256, name.sha384, name.sha512).
   Auto  ONE hash that fits the key: SHA-512 for RSA-8192, ECDSA P-521, ML-DSA-87; SHA-384 for
         RSA-4096, P-384, ML-DSA-65; SHA-256 for the rest.
   Also  sha3-256, sha3-384, sha3-512, or None.
 SHA256SUMS, SHA384SUMS and SHA512SUMS at the top of the folder cover the whole export.

====================================================================================================
 13  RECIPES, COPY AND PASTE
====================================================================================================
 Cloudflare, Q1 OV chain:   .\Export-PkiCa.ps1 -Ca q1-ov -WithPath -Target Cloudflare
 Azure and CloudFront:      .\Export-PkiCa.ps1 -Ca q1-ov -WithPath -Target AzureCdn,CloudFront
 Only the root, all names:  .\Export-PkiCa.ps1 -Ca EnterpriseRoot -Target All
 Normal and NoCR PKI:       .\Export-PkiCa.ps1 -Ca Roots -BothVariants
 Customer certificate:      .\Export-PkiCa.ps1 -Leaf .\www-fullchain.crt -Target All
 Public CA, no PKI here:    .\Export-PkiCa.ps1 -Leaf .\www.crt -NoPki -FetchAia
 Move a CA with its key:    .\Export-PkiCa.ps1 -Ca q1-ov -WithPath -IncludeKey -KeyPassword 'xxx'
 Another folder:            .\Export-PkiCa.ps1 -OutFolder D:\export

====================================================================================================
 14  PROBLEMS AND ANSWERS
====================================================================================================
 "Nothing to export" (exit 2)      The selection is empty. Use -List to see the ids.
 "No PKI found"                    Give -PkiRoot, or -Leaf together with -NoPki.
 "The key ... is not on this       The key of that CA is not here (a DevKit or a transported
  computer"                        PKI). The certificate is still written.
 "issuer not found"                Add the file with -ChainFile, or use -FetchAia.
 The CDN says "invalid CA"         Check: a CA certificate (a leaf is refused), one certificate
                                   per file, PEM text. For ML-DSA see section 11.
 The key file asks for a password  It is encrypted. The password was shown once. Export again with
                                   your own -KeyPassword.
 The Enterprise Root key is        It needs -Force on purpose.
  refused

====================================================================================================
 15  LIMITS YOU MUST KNOW
====================================================================================================
 - It does not talk to any CDN. You upload the files yourself.
 - It cannot make a certificate. For that see SignCsr-MANUAL.txt.
 - A post-quantum CA is not accepted by public CDNs today.
 - -FetchAia needs the network and trusts the address inside the certificate. Check what it
   downloaded.

 16  ALL OPTIONS
====================================================================================================
 -Ca <list>            Which CAs. Default Roots.      -WithPath   -BothVariants   -List
 -Format <list>        PEM CRT CER DER P7B TXT ALL.   -Hash <list>   -Target <list>
 -IncludeKey  -KeyPassword <text>  -Force

 -OpenSslPath  -FipsModuleConfig  -Unblock  -Verbose  -Help
 Exit codes: 0 ok, 1 error, 2 nothing to export.
