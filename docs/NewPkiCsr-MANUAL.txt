 READ THIS FIRST
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 What it is   : A script that asks you questions and makes your private key and a certificate
                request (CSR). You send the CSR to the owner of the PKI. He sends back a
                certificate.
 Who reads it : Anyone who needs a certificate from a PKI that someone else runs: developers, QA,
                IT, customers. No PKI knowledge is needed. Every new word is explained in
                GLOSSARY.txt and GLOSSARY-ENGLISH.txt.
 Time needed  : 10 minutes to read sections 1 to 5. Making a request takes about two minutes.
 You need     : OpenSSL 3.x or 4.x. Prepare-Computer installs it for you.
 Start with   : Section 4 (Quick start).

====================================================================================================
 1  WHAT THIS SCRIPT DOES
====================================================================================================
 You need a certificate. Only the owner of the PKI can make it. But the private key must stay
 with YOU. So you do it in two steps:

     YOUR COMPUTER                                  THE OWNER OF THE PKI
     1. make a private key (.key)   stays here
     2. make a request (.csr)       ----------->    3. checks it, signs it (SignCsr)
     5. install the certificate     <-----------    4. sends the certificate back

 New-PkiCsr.ps1 and new-pki-csr.sh do steps 1 and 2. They ask questions, one after the other.
 Everything is written into a new folder. You send ONLY the .csr. The .key never leaves your
 computer.

 The scripts stand alone. They need only OpenSSL. They do not read or change the certificate
 stores. It does not matter whether the PKI is already installed on your computer. If the trust
 kit installed it, the script says so: "PKI already installed on this computer: agantra".

====================================================================================================
 2  THE WORDS YOU NEED
====================================================================================================
 Private key    A secret file. Whoever has it can pretend to be you. It never leaves your
                computer. Never e-mail it.
 CSR            Certificate Signing Request. A file with your public key and your name. Not
                secret. You send it to the owner.
 Certificate    The answer. It ties your name to your public key. The owner's CA signs it.
 Subject        Your name data in the certificate: CN, company, country ...
 CN             Common Name. For a server: its host name, for example app.example.com.
 SAN            Subject Alternative Name. All the names the certificate is valid for: DNS names,
                IP addresses, e-mail addresses.
 Level          DV, OV or EV. How much of your identity was checked. DV = domain name only.
                OV = company too. EV = company, registration number, country.
 Leaf           A certificate for a server, a person or a program.
 CA request     A request for the certificate of a NEW CA (section 6).
 IANA PEN       Private Enterprise Number. A number your company gets from IANA for free.

 3  BEFORE YOU START
====================================================================================================
 - OpenSSL 3.x or 4.x. If you do not have it: .\Prepare-Computer.ps1 (Windows) or
   ./prepare-computer.sh (Linux). Do it once.
 - Know your data: the host name or name for the CN. For OV and EV also the company name, the
   company registration number, the country. Ask the owner of the PKI which level you need.
 - A computer that is allowed: Windows 11 24H2 or newer, Windows Server 2025 and 2022, Windows
   PowerShell 5.1 or PowerShell 7. Linux: Ubuntu 24+, CentOS Stream / RHEL / Rocky / Oracle 10+,
   SLES / openSUSE 15+. Others: exit 5, nothing is done.
 - The scripts are in every trust-kit zip, in the DevKit and in the transport kit.

====================================================================================================
 4  QUICK START
====================================================================================================
     Windows     .\New-PkiCsr.ps1
     Linux       ./new-pki-csr.sh

 Answer the questions. ENTER skips an optional question. At the end you have this folder:

     csr-requests\<name>_<date>-<time>\

 Send ONLY the .csr file to the owner of the PKI.

====================================================================================================
 5  THE QUESTIONS, ONE BY ONE
====================================================================================================
 Question                What to answer
 ----------------------  ---------------------------------------------------------------------
 Type of request         Leaf (default) or IntermediateCA. See section 6.
 Common name (CN)        Required. The host name of the server, or the name of the certificate.
 Company ID              Your company registration number. It becomes the subject serialNumber.
 Company (O)             The official company name.
 Department (OU)         Your department. Required for a CA request.
 E-mail, phone, person   Contact data.
 Address, city, state    Where the company is.
 Country                 Two capital letters, for example EE, ID, DE.
 Postal code             Optional.
 EV information          Business category (Private Organization, Government Entity, Business
                         Entity, Non-Commercial Entity) and where the company is registered
                         (country, state, locality).
 IANA PEN                Optional. Your company number. Your company policy OID
                         1.3.6.1.4.1.<PEN>.1 goes into the request. Other policy OIDs in a
                         request are ignored by the CA.
 Names (SAN)             Section 7.
 Key and hash            Section 8.

 Every answer can be given as an option instead (section 16). Then that question is not asked.
 Special characters in values (# $ " \) are kept as you type them.

====================================================================================================
 6  LEAF OR CA REQUEST
====================================================================================================
 Leaf (default)     A server, a person, code signing or e-mail certificate. The owner signs it
                    with SignCsr.ps1 / SignCsr.sh. Most people need this.

 IntermediateCA     The certificate of a NEW CA of your department. The request asks for CA:TRUE
                    and the key usage keyCertSign + cRLSign. Rules:
                    - Required: company, country, department (OU), CA name (CN).
                    - It has NO names (no SAN). It can never have the development names.
                    - Key: RSA 4096 or 8192, or ECDSA P-384 or P-521. NOT ML-DSA, NOT P-256, NOT
                      RSA 2048 or 3072.
                    - The owner signs it with SignExternalCa.ps1 (a Quadrant Root, original PKI
                      only).
                    - Keep the key like a root key: offline, or in an HSM.

 The owner's scripts see the kind by themselves (CA:TRUE = CA request). If he starts the wrong
 script, it tells him which one to use.

====================================================================================================
 7  NAMES (SAN) AND DEVELOPMENT NAMES
====================================================================================================
 The SAN lists every name that the certificate must be valid for. The script asks for them one by
 one: DNS names, IP addresses, e-mail addresses. An empty line means finished. If you gave no CN,
 the first DNS name becomes the CN.

 Rules: EV: no wildcard name. Code signing: no names. A CA request: no names.

 "Add the development names?" Answer yes and the script puts a marker name in the request. The
 owner's SignCsr then adds his list of development names (like -AddDevelopmentSAN). The marker
 itself is never in the certificate. Not for a CA request.

====================================================================================================
 8  KEY AND HASH
====================================================================================================
 Default: RSA 4096 with SHA-384. The script accepts:
   RSA 2048, 3072, 4096, 8192          ECDSA P-256, P-384, P-521
   ML-DSA-44, 65, 87 (quantum; needs OpenSSL 3.5 or newer, or 4)
 Code signing needs RSA 4096+, ECDSA P-384+ or ML-DSA-65+. The hash is SHA-256, SHA-384 or
 SHA-512. SHA-1, SHA-224 and MD5 are refused. If you do not know, press ENTER and keep the default.
 An ML-DSA request needs a Quantum CA: tell the owner (he signs with -Quantum).

 9  WHAT IS IN THE NEW FOLDER
====================================================================================================

 File               What it is

====================================================================================================
 10  WHAT THE CERTIFICATE CONTAINS
====================================================================================================
 The owner's CA decides the level from what your request carries. He can force it with -DV, -OV
 or -EV.

 EV information complete (category + jurisdiction + company ID + company + state + city)  -> EV
 A company (name + country), no EV information                                            -> OV
 Only a common name                                                                       -> DV

 Unless he says otherwise: Classic, Quadrant 1. The CRL and OCSP addresses in the certificate are
 those of the CA that signs it. The key usage and extended key usage come from the owner's choice
 (-Purpose), not from your request.

====================================================================================================
 11  AFTER YOU GET THE CERTIFICATE
====================================================================================================
 The owner sends a folder. Put the files next to your .key.
   Web server (nginx)   ssl_certificate web01-fullchain.crt;  ssl_certificate_key web01.key;
   Windows / IIS        import web01.crt with web01.key as a PFX (ask your IT staff)
   Trust                Your clients must trust the PKI. Use the trust kit (PkiTrustKit-MANUAL.txt).
 Check the chain:  openssl verify -CAfile root.crt -untrusted web01-chain.crt web01.crt
 Back up the .key. If you lose it, you must make a new request and a new certificate.

====================================================================================================
 12  RULES THE SCRIPT ENFORCES
====================================================================================================
 The script stops BEFORE it makes a key when you break a rule. The CA checks the same again.
 - RSA below 2048 bit. ECDSA other than P-256, P-384, P-521. ML-DSA other than 44, 65, 87.
 - A request hash below SHA-256.
 - Code signing: RSA 4096+, ECDSA P-384+, ML-DSA-65+.
 - EV: no wildcard name.
 - Country: two capital letters.
 - Company and country go together.
 - A CA request: only the key types of section 6.

====================================================================================================
 13  RECIPES, COPY AND PASTE
====================================================================================================
 DV, a server (nothing is asked):
     .\New-PkiCsr.ps1 -CommonName app.example.com -San app.example.com,10.0.0.5 -NonInteractive
 OV, with the development names:
     .\New-PkiCsr.ps1 -CommonName app.example.com -Company "Example OU" -CompanyId 17344131 `
         -Country EE -San app.example.com -AddDevelopmentSAN -NonInteractive
 EV, ECDSA P-384:
     .\New-PkiCsr.ps1 -CommonName ev.example.com -Company "Example OU" -CompanyId 17344131 `
         -Country EE -State Harju -City Tallinn -Address "Street 1" `
         -BusinessCategory "Private Organization" -JurisdictionCountry EE `
         -San ev.example.com -IanaPen 99999 -KeyType ECDSA -Curve P-384 -NonInteractive
 A CA of your department:
     .\New-PkiCsr.ps1 -CertificateType IntermediateCA -CommonName "Dept Issuing CA G1" `
         -Company "Dept Ltd" -OrganizationalUnit "IT Department" -Country EE -NonInteractive
 Linux, EV:
     ./new-pki-csr.sh --cn ev.example.com --company "Example OU" --company-id 17344131 \
         --country EE --state Harju --city Tallinn --address "Street 1" \
         --business-category "Private Organization" --jurisdiction-country EE \
         --san ev.example.com --pen 99999 --key ecdsa --curve P-384 --non-interactive
 Linux, CA:
     ./new-pki-csr.sh --type intermediate-ca --cn "Dept Issuing CA G1" --company "Dept Ltd" \
         --ou "IT Department" --country EE --non-interactive
 What the owner runs:
     .\SignCsr.ps1 -Csr x.csr          .\SignCsr.ps1 -Csr x.csr -Quantum
     .\SignExternalCa.ps1 -Csr ca.csr  (a CA request; the level switch is not needed)

====================================================================================================
 14  PROBLEMS AND ANSWERS
====================================================================================================
 "OpenSSL 3.x or 4.x was not found" (exit 4)
     Run Prepare-Computer.ps1 / prepare-computer.sh, or give -OpenSslPath / --openssl.
 "This system is not supported" (exit 5)
     See section 3. Nothing was done.
 The script refuses my key
     Section 12. For example RSA 2048 for code signing is too small. Choose RSA 4096.
 "The input ended before all questions were answered"
     You ran it where nobody can type (a pipe, a build job). Give the values as options and add
     -NonInteractive / --non-interactive.
 The owner says "the request is refused"
     Send him request-info.txt. His SignCsr report names the field (SignCsr-MANUAL.txt,
     section 12).
 I lost the .key
     Make a new request. The old certificate is useless without the key. Ask the owner to revoke
     it.
 Windows: "running scripts is disabled"
     powershell -NoProfile -ExecutionPolicy RemoteSigned -File .\New-PkiCsr.ps1

====================================================================================================
 15  LIMITS YOU MUST KNOW
====================================================================================================
 - The script makes the request only. It does not install the certificate.
 - The key file has no password. The script limits it to your user (chmod 600 on Linux, icacls
   on Windows). Also keep the folder on an encrypted disk.
 - The CA decides the level, the usages and the validity. Your request cannot force them.
 - The script does not check that your company data is true. The owner of the PKI checks that
   before he signs an OV or EV request.
 - An ML-DSA request only works with a Quantum CA. A CA request cannot use ML-DSA.

 17  WHAT WAS TESTED, WHAT WAS NOT
====================================================================================================
 Tested on Ubuntu 24.04 with OpenSSL 4.0.3 and PowerShell 7: Leaf and CA requests, DV / OV / EV,
